# Voice-Craft

## What this is

The method for recognising, preserving, and working within a writer's distinctive voice. This talent is the method. The markers themselves live in the fortes that use them (voice-guardian).

## How to use it

When a forte draws on voice-craft, apply the preservation rules and detection patterns below. When deriving new voice markers, follow the derivation protocol.

## Voice components

- **Rhythm:** Sentence lengths vary widely — single-word punches through to 40-plus-word constructions. Short sentences earn impact from contrast. A paragraph of uniformly short or uniformly long sentences flatlines. The pattern is varied, not short.
- **Register:** Formal/informal, technical/accessible, distance from reader.
- **Diction:** Word choices that recur, words the author avoids.
- **Syntax:** How sentences are built. This author's syntax is verb-driven. Sentences move through action — subjects do things, observations land as verbs, arguments advance through what happens. Nominalisations are a compression move, not a structural habit. When noun phrases carry the argument instead of verbs, precision and forward motion are lost.
- **Stance:** Relationship to subject, to reader, to self.

## Detection patterns

1. Read aloud. Where does it not sound like the author?
2. Compare to known samples. What's missing? What's added?
3. Identify signature moves. Are they present?
4. Check for smoothing. Has distinctiveness been filed down?

## Preservation rules

1. Unusual phrasing is voice until proven error.
2. Rough edges may be deliberate.
3. Consistency with the author's other work outweighs style guides.
4. When in doubt, preserve.

## Voice vs error test

- **Voice:** deliberate deviation from convention that creates effect.
- **Error:** unintentional deviation that creates confusion.

The test: Does the author do this elsewhere? Would they defend it? If yes, it's voice.

## You must NOT

1. Correct to standard English when non-standard is voice.
2. Smooth rhythm to "improve flow."
3. Substitute common words for unusual choices.
4. Add transitions the author deliberately omitted.
5. Make prose "clearer" by making it generic.

## Voice derivation protocol

Run this when a new game starts, when markers are flagged as stale, or after every 5-10 new articles.

1. **Read at least five published pieces.** Full articles, not excerpts. The voice lives in sustained passages.
2. **Extract patterns, not examples.** Output: "sentence lengths vary widely" — not a list of sentences. Markers describe how the prose works, not what it says.
3. **Test by absence.** For each candidate: if this pattern were removed, would the prose still sound like the author? If yes, it's a stylistic option, not a voice marker.
4. **Derive negatives from the corpus, not from style guides.** "Not formal register" is only valid if the corpus consistently avoids it.
5. **Record the source.** Each marker traceable to patterns across multiple pieces. A one-off construction is a choice, not a marker.
6. **Preserve what you find.** If the voice has rough edges, those edges are markers. Derivation that smooths what it finds has failed.

## Where markers live

Markers derived here go into the fortes that use them (voice-guardian). Do not duplicate between talent and fortes. This talent is the method for deriving and testing. The fortes carry the results.

## Cross-genre voice sources

The author's voice exists across genres. Blog and novel share roots — flat declarations, comic precision, specificity as authority, verb-driven syntax, earned emotional turns. When deriving blog voice markers, `talents/voice-craft-novel.md` provides additional signal. The novel shows moves the blog also makes: cumulative sentences that pile and undercut, pivot-point flatness, specifics doing work abstractions would fumble. Filter for register (the blog has no characters, no narrator-intrusion gags) but don't discard the shared patterns.
