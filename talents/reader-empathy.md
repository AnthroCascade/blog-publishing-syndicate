# Reader Empathy

How readers process, resist, and respond to arguments. The reader's experience, not the writer's intent.

## Attention economics

Readers don't read — they scan, commit, or abandon. Every paragraph is a decision point.

- **Opening contract:** First two paragraphs establish what the reader gets. Break the contract and they leave.
- **Cognitive budget:** Each abstraction, each unfamiliar term, each long sentence draws down a finite budget. Readers quit when overdrawn.
- **Momentum:** Forward motion comes from unanswered questions, not from smooth prose. A reader who knows where the piece is going has no reason to keep reading.

**Test:** At each paragraph break — why does the reader turn the page?

## Resistance patterns

Where readers push back, and why:

- **Premature universalising:** "Everyone knows..." / "We all..." — the reader who doesn't is now adversarial.
- **Unearned authority:** Claims that require credentials the piece hasn't established.
- **Straw opponents:** Weak versions of counterarguments the reader holds. They notice.
- **Skipped steps:** Conclusions that arrive faster than the reader's reasoning. They feel manipulated, not persuaded.
- **Overclaiming:** Scope larger than the evidence. The reader mentally downgrades everything else.

**Test:** Where would a sympathetic-but-critical reader say "hang on"?

## Recognition and reward

Where readers nod, highlight, or share:

- **Naming the unnamed:** Articulating something the reader has felt but never formulated.
- **Portable frameworks:** Mental models the reader can apply to their own context.
- **Specific surprise:** A concrete detail that reframes something familiar.
- **Permission:** Validating a position the reader holds but hasn't voiced.

**Test:** What does the reader take away that they didn't have before?

## Register fit

The gap between the writer's register and the reader's expectations:

- **Talking up:** Academic register to a practitioner audience. Reader feels excluded.
- **Talking down:** Explaining what the reader already knows. Reader feels patronised.
- **Wrong tribe:** Using one community's shibboleths for another community's audience.

**Test:** Does the reader feel addressed as a peer?

## Completion pressure

What makes a reader finish vs abandon:

- **Investment:** The reader has committed attention and wants payoff.
- **Tension:** An unresolved question the reader needs answered.
- **Pace:** The ratio of insight to words. Thin stretches lose readers.
- **Length signal:** The reader estimates time remaining. If the remaining content doesn't justify the remaining length, they skim or quit.

**Test:** At the halfway point — is the reader still earning enough to stay?

## For evaluative fortes

Flag: premature universalising, unearned authority claims, straw opponents, skipped reasoning steps, overclaiming, register mismatch (up or down), thin stretches, broken opening contracts, missing portable insight.

## For the drafter

Opening contract in the first two paragraphs. Earn authority before wielding it. Pace insight delivery — no thin stretches. Name the unnamed. Give the reader something portable.
