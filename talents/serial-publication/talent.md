# Serial Publication

## Domain

How articles in a series relate to each other. Intellectual progression across pieces. What the reader carries forward.

## Optimises for

Continuity of thought across publications. Each piece advances the project. The reader's accumulated understanding is an asset, not an obstacle.

## The reader's carry-forward

A series reader arrives with accumulated context:

- Concepts already introduced and argued
- Frameworks already established
- Positions the author has taken
- Vocabulary the author has coined or adopted
- Questions left open in previous pieces

This carry-forward is real. Ignoring it wastes the reader's investment. Repeating what they already know is padding. Contradicting it without acknowledgment is incoherent.

## Positioning patterns

**Extends:** This article takes a concept from a previous piece and applies it to new territory. The reader needs the earlier concept but not the full earlier argument.

**Resolves:** This article answers a question left open in a previous piece. The reader has been carrying the question. Naming it creates recognition.

**Instantiates:** This article shows a specific case of a general principle established elsewhere. The general principle is assumed; the specific case is new.

**Complicates:** This article reveals something that makes a previous claim harder to hold. The reader's understanding must update.

**Converges:** This article brings together threads from multiple previous pieces. The reader sees connections they may not have made.

## Callback patterns

**Naming without re-explaining:** Reference a concept by its established name. The reader who knows it recognises it. The reader who doesn't can follow the link. Do not re-derive.

**Advancing, not restating:** When picking up a thread, start where the previous article left off, not where it began. "The friction paradox — which governs..." not "In a previous article, I argued that..."

**Tension with new material:** The strongest callbacks create productive tension. The earlier concept seemed complete; this article shows it wasn't.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating each article as standalone when the reader isn't
- Re-explaining concepts the reader already has (padding in series context)
- Referencing previous articles for authority rather than building ("As I argued in...")
- Assuming the reader remembers details — reference the concept, not the article
- Breaking continuity without acknowledging the break

## Scope and function

An article's position in a series has both scope and function:

- **Scope:** Where it sits in the intellectual trajectory. What came before. What it enables after.
- **Function:** What work it does for the series. Introduces, extends, resolves, complicates, converges.

Neither is determined by the article's content alone. The author decides where a piece sits and what work it does.

## Boundary

What serial publication IS: understanding how articles relate across a body of work. Continuity, progression, accumulated reader context.

What serial publication is NOT: content strategy, audience growth, publication scheduling. Those are operational. This is intellectual.
