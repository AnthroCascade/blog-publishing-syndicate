# Influence

Cialdini's seven principles applied to this author's register and structure.

## The author's influence patterns

### Authority — demonstrated practice

**Generate:** Conversational register. Specific claims carry their own authority.

**Evaluate:** Flag prose positioning the reader as student.

### Liking — conversational register, vulnerability

**Generate:** Maintain conversational register throughout.

**Evaluate:** Flag formal register shifts.

### Unity — practitioner to practitioner

**Generate:** Observe with the reader: "Here's what happens when you..." Position as peer.

**Evaluate:** Flag passages positioning the reader as student.

### Commitment — sequence easy to hard

**Generate:** Sequence claims so the reader commits to the easier position first. Arrive at the contentious claim.

**Evaluate:** Flag contentious claims placed before commitment is built.

### Social proof — recognition or scarcity

**Generate:** Citations serve recognition ("yes, I've seen that") or scarcity ("nobody is saying this"). Cut the rest.

**Evaluate:** Flag decorative citations.

### Scarcity — the rare insight

**Generate:** Open with the insight unavailable elsewhere. Deliver fast.

**Evaluate:** Flag prose anyone could have written from the same sources.

### Reciprocity — portable insight

**Generate:** Every section gives the reader something concrete — a term, a framework, an observation.

**Evaluate:** Flag sections with no portable insight.

## For the drafter

Conversational register. Easy-to-hard sequencing. Citations serve recognition or scarcity. Every section gives the reader something portable.

## For evaluative fortes

Flag: formal register, reader-as-student positioning, contentious claims before commitment, decorative citations, absent scarcity, thin reciprocity.
